FIRE AND SMOKE DISASTER RESPONSE PROTOCOL
(Adapted from Pakistan's National Disaster Management Authority response framework)

RISK FACTORS AND TRIGGERS
Fire risk (structural, forest, or industrial) increases significantly under:
- Low humidity and high temperature conditions
- Dry vegetation combined with wind (for forest/wildland fires)
- Electrical faults or poor safety compliance in industrial/urban settings
- Absence of recent rainfall, which allows fuel sources (vegetation, debris) to dry out
Fire risk is generally reduced in conditions of high humidity or active rainfall, which should be factored into verification of any fire detection.

EARLY WARNING THRESHOLDS
- Watch (Level 1): Weather conditions indicate elevated fire risk (high heat, low humidity, dry spell). Action: issue public advisory, alert fire services to be on standby.
- Alert (Level 2): Smoke detected via monitoring (satellite, camera network, or reported sighting) but not yet confirmed as active fire, or a confirmed small fire within control. Action: dispatch verification team or nearest fire unit, prepare evacuation routes for nearby structures if applicable.
- Evacuate (Level 3): Active, spreading fire confirmed, threatening structures or populated areas. Action: full evacuation of at-risk zone, full fire service deployment, activation of emergency operations center.

RESPONSE MECHANISM (TIERED ACTIVATION)
Tier 1 - Local Response: Local fire services and district disaster authorities handle contained fires within normal operational capacity.
Tier 2 - Provincial Response: Activated when a fire exceeds local suppression capacity, spans multiple jurisdictions, or threatens critical infrastructure. Provincial assets (additional firefighting units, aerial water/retardant drops where available) are mobilized.
Tier 3 - National Response: Activated for large-scale wildland fires or major industrial/urban fires affecting multiple provinces or requiring specialized national assets (military aviation support, cross-border assistance).

FIRE SUPPRESSION AND SAFETY PROCEDURES
- Establish a safe perimeter and evacuate all non-essential personnel before suppression efforts begin.
- For wildland/forest fires, prioritize creating firebreaks ahead of the fire's projected path, factoring in wind direction and speed.
- For structural/industrial fires, coordinate with utility providers to cut gas and electrical supply to the affected area before entry.
- Use thermal imaging drones where available to detect hotspots not visible to ground teams and to monitor for fire spread direction.
- Maintain communication with meteorological services for real-time wind and humidity updates, which directly affect fire behavior.

IMMEDIATE RESPONSE CHECKLIST
1. Confirm the detection and assess scale and spread rate.
2. Activate the appropriate response tier based on scale and threat to structures/population.
3. Establish an incident command post upwind of the fire.
4. Deploy fire suppression teams and evacuate at-risk structures/areas.
5. Set up a casualty collection and triage point for smoke inhalation or burn injuries.
6. Coordinate road closures and evacuation routes with traffic authorities.
7. Monitor air quality downwind for smoke-related public health advisories.
8. Establish relief point for displaced residents.
9. Once contained, conduct hotspot monitoring for 24-48 hours to prevent flare-ups.

RECOVERY CONSIDERATIONS
- Conduct structural safety assessments before allowing residents to return to fire-affected buildings.
- For wildland fires, assess soil stability on burned slopes, since vegetation loss can increase landslide risk in subsequent rainfall.
- Conduct a post-event review to identify ignition cause and update prevention guidance.

VULNERABLE GROUPS
Special consideration should be given to residents with respiratory conditions during smoke exposure events, as well as elderly residents and young children who are more susceptible to smoke inhalation effects.
