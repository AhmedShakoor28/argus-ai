LANDSLIDE DISASTER RESPONSE PROTOCOL
(Adapted from Pakistan's National Disaster Management Authority response framework)

RISK FACTORS AND TRIGGERS
Landslides in mountainous regions are typically triggered by one or more of the following:
- Heavy or prolonged rainfall saturating soil on slopes
- Earthquakes destabilizing slope material
- Rapid snowmelt combined with saturated ground
- Human activity such as road construction or deforestation on unstable slopes
Regions with steep terrain, loose sedimentary rock, and monsoon rainfall exposure carry elevated baseline risk.

EARLY WARNING THRESHOLDS
A tiered warning system should be used:
- Watch (Level 1): Rainfall forecast exceeding safe thresholds for the region, or satellite imagery showing ground movement precursors. Action: issue public advisory, alert local authorities, pre-check emergency stocks.
- Alert (Level 2): Confirmed heavy rainfall in progress, or visible slope cracking/movement reported. Action: partial activation of emergency response teams, pre-position rescue equipment, begin evacuation of highest-risk households.
- Evacuate (Level 3): Active landslide detected or imminent failure confirmed by field observation or automated detection. Action: full evacuation of at-risk zone, deployment of search and rescue teams, activation of emergency operations center.

RESPONSE MECHANISM (TIERED ACTIVATION)
Tier 1 - Local Response: Managed by district-level disaster authorities for small, contained incidents. Includes immediate search and rescue, first aid, and evacuation of the directly affected community.
Tier 2 - Provincial Response: Activated when the incident exceeds local capacity, e.g. blocks a major road or affects multiple communities. Provincial teams mobilize additional rescue assets, medical support, and temporary shelter.
Tier 3 - National Response: Activated for large-scale events affecting multiple districts or requiring specialized equipment (heavy machinery for debris clearance, helicopter evacuation). National-level coordination and, where needed, international assistance requests occur at this tier.

SEARCH AND RESCUE PROCEDURES
- Deploy trained search and rescue teams equipped with rope rescue gear, thermal imaging (for locating buried survivors), and debris-clearing tools.
- Establish a safe perimeter around the slide area before rescue operations begin, since secondary slides are common in the hours following an initial event.
- Use drones or aerial reconnaissance where available to assess the full extent of the slide and identify additional at-risk areas nearby.
- Coordinate with local community volunteers who often have the fastest initial response time before official teams arrive.

IMMEDIATE RESPONSE CHECKLIST
1. Confirm the event and assess scale (number of structures/people affected, blocked routes).
2. Activate appropriate response tier based on scale.
3. Establish an incident command post near the affected area.
4. Deploy search and rescue teams with appropriate equipment for terrain.
5. Evacuate any structures still at risk of secondary slides.
6. Set up a casualty collection and triage point.
7. Coordinate with meteorological services to monitor for continued rainfall that could trigger further slides.
8. Establish relief distribution point for displaced residents (water, shelter, medical care).
9. Begin damage assessment for infrastructure (roads, utilities) once the area is declared safe.

RECOVERY CONSIDERATIONS
- Assess whether the affected slope remains unstable before allowing residents to return.
- Consider "Build Back Better" principles: any reconstruction should account for the landslide risk that caused the original event, potentially relocating structures away from the highest-risk zones.
- Conduct a post-event review to document lessons learned and update local hazard maps.

VULNERABLE GROUPS
Special consideration should be given during evacuation and relief to elderly residents, persons with disabilities, and families with young children, who may need additional assistance evacuating steep or damaged terrain.
